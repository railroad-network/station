# Security Audit — August 2026

**Repository:** `station` (Railroad Network reference implementation)
**Commit audited:** `f59271cbc6683d1d1be838606d18ea5ae9b5e76c` (2026-08-25)
**Performed by:** Claude (Anthropic AI model, Fable 5), operated by the project maintainer
**Date:** 2026-08-25

## Disclosure and limitations

This audit was performed by an AI model, not by a professional security firm. It
is an internal review intended to raise the floor, not an attestation. Treat its
conclusions accordingly:

- It is a **code review**, not a penetration test: no fuzzing campaigns, no
  side-channel measurement, no adversarial tooling was run against a live
  deployment.
- **Absence of a finding is not evidence of absence.** An AI reviewer can miss
  bugs a human specialist would catch, and vice versa.
- Findings were verified against the code at the commit above; each carries a
  concrete failure scenario rather than a hypothetical.
- A professional audit is still warranted before any deployment where real
  people depend on this software's guarantees — particularly given the
  privacy-sensitive user population the project's own framing implies.

## Scope and methodology

Reviewed in full: `rrn-crypto` (all modules), `rrn-identity` (wallet, sealed
box, dual-wrap envelope, address, attestation/vouch, all of `recovery/`),
`rrn-storage` (db, log, replay), `rrn-ledger` (transaction, engine, settlement),
`rrn-station` (rpc_envelope, pairing, recovery, backup, gossip, config, and the
authentication/dispatch/authorization paths of `core.rs`), and
`rrn-mobile-ffi/recovery`. Reviewed by targeted inspection: `rrn-dispute`
(sortition, resolution), `rrn-governance` (charter, tally), `rrn-reputation`
(sybil, staking), CLI/daemon passphrase handling. Cross-cutting checks: every
`SignedPayload` path for signer-binding and domain separation, deserialization
of untrusted bytes, `unsafe` inventory (none outside lint declarations),
`cargo audit` / `cargo deny check advisories` (clean; two allowed warnings, see
RRN-A-008). The existing `docs/threat-model.md` was checked against the code it
describes.

Severity scale: **High** (practical compromise of funds, keys, or identity),
**Medium** (compromise requiring social engineering, unusual position, or
combination with another flaw; or a protocol property weaker than documented),
**Low** (hardening gaps, defense-in-depth), **Info** (observations,
accepted-risk confirmations).

## Summary

No High-severity findings. The cryptographic core is in notably good shape:
correct primitive choices consistently applied, strict signature verification,
canonical-by-construction serialization with domain-separation tags in every
signed payload, disciplined zeroization, and a Shamir implementation that
matches its ADR and is tested against an independent reference. The findings
below are concentrated at the *protocol and exposure* level — what the network
surfaces serve, what the ceremony flows assume about humans, and gaps between
the threat model and the newest code.

## Findings

### RRN-A-001 · Medium — Recovery ceremony requests are unauthenticated

**Where:** `rrn-identity/src/recovery/ceremony.rs`,
`rrn-mobile-ffi/src/recovery.rs` (`respond_to_recovery`),
`rrn-station/src/recovery.rs` (`begin_restore`), and a gap in
`docs/threat-model.md`.

A `RecoveryRequest` is `{recovery_pubkey, target_address}` with **no
authentication of the requester** — necessarily, since the legitimate requester
has lost their key. Anyone can mint an ephemeral keypair and construct a valid
request targeting any address. If an attacker persuades `K` holders to scan
their `rrnrecover-req:` QR and return responses, `finish_restore` hands the
attacker the victim's exact secret key — full identity takeover, with no
cryptographic step anywhere that could have stopped it.

**Failure scenario.** A community uses 2-of-3 recovery. An attacker who knows
the holder set (the persisted `recovery.rrnrecovery` package is plaintext
metadata, and holder addresses are visible to whoever reads it) approaches two
holders separately — "Aaron lost his phone, he asked me to collect this, here's
the QR" — and each holder's device happily produces a sealed response, because
`build_response` checks only that the shard matches the *target address in the
attacker's own request*.

**What exists already.** The mobile FFI exposes `parse_recovery_request` so the
app can show a confirm screen, and the ceremony is designed as an in-person QR
ritual. The `AddressMismatch` check stops a holder contributing to the *wrong*
identity, but nothing addresses a *forged requester* for the right identity.

**Why this matters more than usual here.** The threat model's recovery section
(`rrn-identity::recovery`) predates the ceremony (T1.11.3) and covers only the
package/shard layer; grep confirms no section covers `rrnrecover-req:`/`-resp:`
at all. The strongest available mitigation is procedural, and procedures that
are not written down do not survive contact with a real community.

**Recommendation.**
1. Add a threat-model section for the reconstruction ceremony naming this as
   the residual risk it is, with the holder-verification procedure stated
   normatively ("verify the requester's identity in person or by a channel you
   already trust; a recovery request is exactly as trustworthy as the person
   showing it to you").
2. Require the holder-side UI (mobile confirm screen) to display strong warning
   copy plus a short fingerprint of `recovery_pubkey`, so two holders talking
   to each other can detect that they were shown *different* ceremonies.
3. Consider protocol hardening for a later phase: e.g., a holder's response
   could be released only after `M` holders co-acknowledge the same
   `recovery_pubkey` fingerprint, or after a mandatory delay during which the
   ceremony is announced on the community log (giving the real owner — who may
   still have a working device — a window to object).

### RRN-A-002 · Medium — Sortition seed is grindable and previewable

**Where:** `rrn-dispute/src/sortition.rs:63` (`sortition_seed`),
`rrn-dispute/src/resolution.rs`.

The jury draw is deliberately deterministic (ADR-0014 §2): the seed is
`Blake3(domain ‖ tx_id ‖ anchor)`. But `tx_id` is the content address of the
*proposal*, and the proposal's sender freely chooses several of its fields
(`memo`, `expires_at`, and to a degree `proposed_at`). The anchor (genesis
Charter hash) is public and fixed. Two consequences:

1. **Grinding.** A sender planning a transaction they may later need to win a
   dispute over can iterate memo bytes offline at hash speed, computing the full
   draw sequence for each candidate `tx_id` until one seats a jury front-loaded
   with allies (the eligible pool and weights are readable from the log). The
   voucher-recusal rule removes the sender's own vouchers, but not friendly
   parties outside that edge.
2. **Adverse selection by the raiser.** Either party can compute the exact jury
   *before deciding whether to dispute*, and simply not dispute (or settle out
   of band, or time their dispute differently) when the draw is unfavorable.
   The dispute system's deterrent value degrades when its outcome is knowable
   in advance.

Neither ADR-0014 nor the threat model acknowledges seed grinding or jury
preview.

**Recommendation.** Document the property at minimum. To actually close the
grinding half, mix into the seed a value that is (a) fixed only *after* the
proposal exists and (b) outside any single party's control — e.g. the content
hash of the *dispute record* combined with the log's tail hash at the moment the
dispute is accepted. (The raiser controls their own record, so use both parties'
records or a later anchor; full unpredictability likely needs a commit-reveal or
a Phase 2 federation beacon — worth an ADR.) Jury *preview* by the raiser is
harder to remove in a replayable-by-design system and may be an accepted cost;
say so in writing.

### RRN-A-003 · Medium — Peer gossip port serves the entire log to anyone

**Where:** `rrn-station/src/gossip.rs` (`serve_peers`, `dispatch_peer`),
`docs/threat-model.md` daemon section.

The peer TCP surface answers `log_range { from_seq: 1, to_seq: tail }` for any
client that connects — no authentication, no allowlist check against the
configured peer list, no transport encryption. The full log contains every
balance-deriving settlement, every vouch (who knows whom), transaction memos,
marketplace history, and dispute statements.

The threat model covers this surface's spoofing/tampering/DoS exposure well and
accepts several risks explicitly — but **information disclosure via the peer
port is not among them**. The listed disclosure threats cover the in-memory key
and IPC socket only. For a project whose design language is the Underground
Railroad, "anyone who can reach TCP port N can download the complete social
graph and transaction history of the community" is the kind of property that
must be a deliberate, written decision, not an accident of the Phase 0 stub.

**Failure scenario.** A station operator, following the mobile listener's
example (`0.0.0.0:7500` is the *default* mobile bind in `config.rs`), configures
`network.listen` on a routable interface so a peer community can reach it. Any
third party on that network path — no key, no membership — pulls the whole log
with a 5-line script.

**Recommendation.** Short-term: add the disclosure threat to the threat model
as accepted-for-Phase-0/1, and have `serve_peers` reject connections from
addresses not in the configured peer list (weak, but cheap and honest about
intent). Real fix rides with the Phase 2 transport (ADR-0013 / Reticulum),
which should authenticate peers before serving `log_range`. Documentation for
operators should state plainly what the gossip port exposes.

### RRN-A-004 · Low — Any paired mobile can read any member's ledger view

**Where:** `rrn-station/src/core.rs` — `m_balance` (:724), `m_transactions`
(:852), `m_next_nonce` (:872); reachable by mobiles via the read-method
passthrough in `route_channel_method` (:3267).

The channel's write methods are exemplary about binding the signed record to the
authenticated mobile. The read methods are not scoped at all: `balance`,
`transactions`, and `next_nonce` take an arbitrary `address` parameter, so any
paired member can enumerate any other member's balance and full per-transaction
history (amounts, counterparties, memos) from their phone.

Within a fully-replicating community log this may be a deliberate transparency
property — every replica holds the data anyway — but it is a *policy* choice
that deserves to be explicit, and phone-reachable convenience APIs lower the bar
from "runs a replica" to "taps a button".

**Recommendation.** Either scope the mobile-reachable read methods to
`envelope.signer`'s own address (operators retain the unscoped view via the
local socket), or record in the threat model / design doc that intra-community
ledger transparency is intended, so the pilot community can be told.

### RRN-A-005 · Low — Log tamper-evidence is never exercised at runtime

**Where:** `rrn-storage/src/log.rs` (`verify_chain`), station startup
(`rrn-station/src/station.rs` / `core.rs`).

`verify_chain` exists, is correct, and is tested — and is never called outside
its own crate's tests. The daemon neither verifies the chain at startup nor
periodically. Additionally, `verify_chain` checks hashes but not per-entry
*signatures*, and replay (`rrn-storage/src/replay.rs`, plus the station's view
builders) decodes stored payload bytes without re-verifying them — all
verification happens at append time only.

**Failure scenario.** Anything that corrupts or modifies `station.db` after
append — filesystem fault, restore of a tampered backup, or an attacker with
file access who rewrites an entry *and* recomputes the downstream hashes (there
is no external anchor, so a full re-chain is internally consistent) — feeds
replay silently. Even the naive tampering the hash chain *would* catch goes
unnoticed, because nothing looks.

**Recommendation.** Run `verify_chain` at station startup (Phase 0 logs are
small; the O(N) cost is fine) and after any restore-from-backup. Extend it (or
add a `verify_chain_deep`) to also verify each entry's stored signature. Note in
the threat model that without cross-replica comparison, a *full* re-chain by an
attacker with DB write access remains undetectable locally — that honesty is
what the device-trust assumption is carrying.

### RRN-A-006 · Low — Wallet temp file briefly world-readable

**Where:** `rrn-identity/src/wallet.rs:421` (`write_private`).

`File::create(path)` creates the `.tmp` file with `0o666 & ~umask` (typically
`0o644`), and `set_permissions(0o600)` lands afterwards. Between the two calls
— and for the duration of the write on a filesystem where another local user
can open the file in that window — the wallet bytes are readable by other local
users. The contents are argon2id+AEAD encrypted, so exposure is of ciphertext,
but the file's entire purpose is `0o600`; the race is free to remove.

**Recommendation.** Create with the mode atomically:
`OpenOptions::new().write(true).create_new(true).mode(0o600)`. The same pattern
applies to the backup archive written by `backup.rs` (`std::fs::write`), which
is also security-relevant ciphertext, and to `RecoveryPackage::save_to_file`
(plaintext metadata; lower stakes, same one-line fix).

### RRN-A-007 · Low — No credit limit anywhere in the ledger

**Where:** `rrn-ledger/src/settlement.rs` (`BalanceView` documents "may be
negative — members can hold debt"), `engine.rs` (`submit_proposal` checks tier,
window, nonce — never balance).

Mutual credit systems conventionally bound how far negative a member may go;
this ledger has no bound at any layer. A member (or a compromised member key)
can propose unlimited Tier-1/Tier-2 transactions and, with willing or colluding
confirmers, accumulate arbitrarily negative balance — extracting real goods and
services from the community before any human reacts. The per-transaction tier
ceilings bound the *size* of each transaction, not the *sum*; the settlement
window delays but does not limit.

This looks like a known Phase 1 scope choice (the reputation system is the
implied brake), but the design doc's economics section should be the authority.
If a credit floor is planned for a later milestone, the threat model should say
that today's build has none; if the reputation/velocity system is intended to be
the only brake, note that velocity checks *flag and do not block*
(`rrn-reputation/src/sybil.rs`, "Flag, do not punish").

**Recommendation.** Decide and document. A simple protocol-level floor
(reject settlement — or better, confirmation — that would push the sender below
a Charter-configurable negative limit) is small to implement in
`Engine::submit_confirmation` and closes the unbounded case for the pilot.

### RRN-A-008 · Low — `lru` unsoundness advisory

`cargo audit` reports RUSTSEC-2026-0253 (potential use-after-free on panic in
`LruCache::pop`) against `lru 0.16.4`, currently an allowed warning alongside
RUSTSEC-2024-0436 (`paste` unmaintained). The `lru` issue is memory-unsafety,
not just staleness; upgrade to a fixed release when one is available and keep
the allowlist entry annotated with a revisit date.

### RRN-A-009 · Info — No X25519 contributory-behavior check

`rrn-identity/src/sealed.rs` performs ECDH without checking
`SharedSecret::was_contributory()`. An attacker-supplied low-order ephemeral
point yields an all-zero shared secret — but in this construction that only
lets an attacker author a box whose key the attacker also knows, which is
equivalent to sending plaintext; it does not affect other messages or the
recipient's key. No action needed; recorded so a future reuse of `sealed` in a
context with different assumptions (e.g. mutual-auth handshakes) re-evaluates.

### RRN-A-010 · Info — Channel response binds to request by nonce only

`ResponseEnvelope` echoes the request `nonce` but not a request hash or method.
Safe today because transport nonces are strictly increasing per mobile and
persisted across restarts (`paired.rs`), and stale responses also fail the
±300 s timestamp bound on the request side. Two things keep this safe and should
stay true: nonce state must never reset without re-pairing, and the skew window
must stay short. A future refactor could bind the response to
`Blake3(request payload)` for free.

### RRN-A-011 · Info — Plaintext memos in a permanent, replicated log

Transaction memos, vouch statements, dispute reasons/statements, and
marketplace text are stored in the clear in an append-only log that is designed
to replicate across stations (and, per RRN-A-003, is currently readable off the
wire). Nothing is wrong with the implementation; the observation is about
guidance: for this project's intended users, the UI and operator documentation
should actively discourage identifying details in memo fields, and the
federation design should treat field-level encryption of free-text as an open
question before Phase 2 replication widens the audience. Dispute records
already bound their lengths and support `evidence_hash` (hash, not content) —
good precedents.

### RRN-A-012 · Info — Smaller observations

- **`now_secs()` fallback** (several crates): on a pre-epoch clock the helpers
  silently return `0` rather than erroring. Harmless on sane hosts; on an
  embedded/mesh deployment with no RTC this would quietly produce records dated
  1970 that pass some window checks. Consider failing loudly.
- **`split_with_coefficients` is `pub`** (`shamir.rs`): documented as a test
  seam with a stated obligation (coefficients must be unpredictable). Fine, but
  it is the one API in the crate whose misuse destroys Shamir's security;
  consider `#[doc(hidden)]` or a `for_tests`-flavored name so it never
  autocompletes into production code.
- **GF(256) table-lookup cache timing**: the threat model documents and accepts
  this residual channel under the local-execution model. Concur with the
  analysis; the branchless `subtle` handling of the zero case is correct.
- **Backup archive integrity**: `DualWrapEnvelope` provides confidentiality and
  per-AEAD integrity but is not signed as a whole; a tampered archive fails
  closed (AEAD errors) rather than restoring wrong data. Verified — no action.
- **Attestation `expires_at` decode leniency** (`attestation.rs`): a wrong-typed
  or absent `expires_at` decodes to `None`, but any such variant re-encodes to
  different bytes and fails signature verification — fail-safe. No action.

## What was checked and found sound

Recorded so the next reviewer does not re-derive it:

- **Signature discipline.** `verify_strict` everywhere (rejects malleable `S`
  and small-order keys); every mobile-submitted write is bound to the
  authenticated channel signer *and* independently signature-checked against
  the identity named inside the record; `MultiSignedPayload` rejects duplicate
  signers and count mismatches; pairing signatures are domain-tagged
  (`rrn-pair-req-v1`/`-resp-v1`).
- **Canonicalization.** dCBOR is canonical by construction; non-canonical input
  is rejected on decode; every signed record embeds a `kind` discriminant
  (`rrn.tx.proposal`, `"vouch"`, etc.), so cross-type signature replay is
  structurally blocked; content-addressed `TransactionId` is recomputed on
  decode and cannot disagree with the bytes.
- **Replay protection.** Per-sender ledger nonces (exact-next, no gaps),
  per-mobile transport nonces (strictly increasing, persisted before dispatch),
  ±300 s timestamp windows, proposal-id dedup, idempotent settlement with the
  already-settled check before any balance write, and content-hash dedup on
  gossip ingest.
- **Key handling.** Secrets zeroized on drop and after use (wallet, shards,
  DEKs, derived keys); no serde on `SecretKey`; redacted `Debug`; argon2id at
  64 MiB/3/4 with per-wallet salt; XChaCha20-Poly1305 with fresh random
  24-byte nonces throughout; ephemeral keys per sealed box and per recovery
  ceremony; distinct blake3 KDF contexts for every sealing purpose.
- **Shamir.** Field arithmetic exhaustively tested against an independent
  reference multiply; index 0 forbidden on both paths; duplicate indices
  rejected; K-of-N and K−1 properties property-tested; reconstruction verified
  against the target address so wrong/insufficient shards fail closed; old and
  new shards cannot combine across a refresh.
- **Storage.** All SQL parameterized; signatures verified before append on both
  the local and replication paths; WAL + foreign keys on every connection;
  `VACUUM INTO` snapshots for consistent live backups.
- **Dependencies.** `cargo audit`/`cargo deny` clean apart from the two allowed
  warnings noted in RRN-A-008.
